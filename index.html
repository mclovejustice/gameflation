WHY YOUR $50 ISN'T $50 ANYMORE
A plain-english guide to inflation and gaming costs
THE CORE IDEA
Imagine a magic shrinking machine that quietly makes your money worth less every year.

Your $50 note still says $50. But each year it buys you slightly less stuff — smaller shopping, fewer litres of petrol, a shorter taxi ride.

That's inflation. Over 40 years it adds up to something enormous.
$50 in 1985 × 3.2 = $160 in 2026
THE SAME PURCHASING POWER — 41 YEARS APART
So when we say Super Mario Bros cost $29.99 in 1985, in today's money that's closer to $96. For a single cartridge.
HOW WE MEASURE IT: THE BASKET
Governments use the Consumer Price Index (CPI) — they track the price of a fixed "basket" of everyday things. When the basket costs more than last year, that rise is the inflation rate.
